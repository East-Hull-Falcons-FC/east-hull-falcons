If a child is in immediate danger

Call the police on 999. Don't wait to speak to the club first.

For an urgent concern about a child in Hull, you can also call children's social care:

  • Early Help and Safeguarding Hub (EHASH): 01482 448 879 option 4
  • Emergency Duty Team (out of hours): 01482 300 304

Once the child is safe, let Tom or Eleanor know so the club can follow up.

Worried about a child? Tell us

For any worry about a child's safety or wellbeing at East Hull Falcons, contact Tom or Eleanor. No concern is too small, and you don't need proof.

Tom is the first contact for the U10s and U11s. You can go straight to Eleanor at any time, and you should if your concern involves Tom or a team he coaches. Both are DBS-checked and have completed the FA's safeguarding and welfare officer training.

You can also speak to your team's coach at any session. Our coaches are DBS-checked and safeguarding-trained, and will pass your concern to Tom or Eleanor the same day. If your concern is about a coach, go straight to Tom or Eleanor, or use the contacts below.

Please don't raise concerns in the WhatsApp community or team chats. Contact Tom or Eleanor directly so the details stay private.

If you can't reach them, or the concern involves the club

You can go outside the club at any time. Use these contacts if you can't reach Tom or Eleanor, if your concern is about either of them or someone close to them, or if you feel the club hasn't acted.

  • East Riding County FA Designated Safeguarding Officer safeguarding@eastridingfa.com Football's local safeguarding lead, if you would rather not go through the club.
  • The FA Safeguarding Team 0800 169 1863 (leave a message) Safeguarding@TheFA.com The national body, or if the usual routes haven't worked (whistleblowing).
  • NSPCC Helpline 0808 800 5000 help@nspcc.org.uk If it's urgent and you can't reach the club or County FA, or you want independent advice.
  • Hull children's social care (EHASH) 01482 448 879 option 4 If your worry is about a child's safety at home or outside football.

What happens after you raise a concern

  1. Whoever you contact listens and writes down what you tell them, in your own words.
  2. They reply within 24 hours to confirm they have it.
  3. Tom and Eleanor share concerns with each other so the club keeps one record, unless the concern involves one of them.
  4. Eleanor, as Club Welfare Officer, decides whether the club can deal with it or whether it must be referred on, taking advice from the County FA when needed.
  5. Serious concerns go to the County FA, The FA, the police or children's social care. The club does not investigate these itself.
  6. You are told as much as possible about what happens next.

What you share is kept private and passed only to people who need it to keep a child safe. Records are stored securely by Eleanor, not in club chats or shared inboxes.

If you're a player

If anything at football makes you feel worried, upset, scared or uncomfortable, tell a grown-up you trust. It is never your fault and you won't be in trouble for speaking up.

  • Tell your mum, dad or carer, or tell your coach.
  • Tom and Eleanor are the club's safeguarding contacts. Their job is to help keep everyone at the club safe and happy. Your coach or parent can help you talk to them.
  • You can tell them about something happening to you or to a friend.
  • If you'd rather talk to someone outside the club, call Childline free on 0800 1111 at any time.
  • If someone online makes you uncomfortable, you can report it to CEOP.

If a child tells you something

Stay calm and listen. Your job is to pass it on, not to work out whether it's true.

  • Reassure them they have done the right thing by telling you.
  • Explain that you need to tell someone who can help. Don't promise to keep it secret.
  • Don't ask leading questions, investigate, or confront anyone.
  • Write down what was said as soon as you can, using the child's own words.
  • Phone or email Tom or Eleanor the same day, even if neither was at the session, or use the outside contacts above.
