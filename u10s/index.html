Matchday information

Getting to the pitch

Spectators' code of conduct

We ask everyone watching to:

  • Remember that children play for fun.
  • Applaud effort and good play as well as success.
  • Respect the referee's decisions, even when you don't agree with them.
  • Appreciate good play from whichever team it comes from.
  • Stay behind the touchline and within the designated spectators' area.
  • Let the coaches do their job and not confuse the players by telling them what to do.
  • Encourage the players to respect the opposition, referee and match officials.
  • Support positively, with encouragement, not criticism.
  • Never use or tolerate offensive, insulting or abusive language or behaviour.

In an emergency

Each of our teams has a qualified first aider, who will introduce themselves to the visiting team before kick-off and takes charge of any serious incident. Visiting teams should bring their own first aider for minor injuries.

How we play6 pages

We want confident footballers who can use both feet, are brave on the ball and try to keep it, whatever the pressure. We always encourage players to try, with the freedom to make mistakes: we are looking at long-term development, not the finished article right now.

How we coach3 pages

Our coaching principles: 90% of everything we do involves a football; we replicate the match pitch in training; and everything we do connects back to the game and matchday. Sessions balance constant, variable and random practice depending on the focus and the children's current level.

How you can help us and themTop tips for parents

At training

  • Make sure they show the values and behaviours we want to see.
  • Let the coaches coach, and don't contradict the messages we give.
  • Trust the process: we're developing a lasting love of football, not just a team for the weekend.
  • Don't focus on mistakes. We're here to build confidence and resilience.
  • Watch your body language and how you talk to them if they look to you.
  • Every child develops at their own pace. Don't judge yours or anyone else's.

On matchday

  • Don't be a touchline coach. They aren't players on a PlayStation.
  • Remember they may not yet be able to do what we're all thinking.
  • Praise effort. At this age it matters more than execution.
  • Accept mistakes positively and encourage them to try again.
  • If the coach is quiet, that's deliberate: it lets the children make their own decisions.
  • Follow our codes of conduct and help make it welcoming for everyone.

At home

  • Ask how it made them feel and what went well, not about the result or a missed chance.
  • Support them emotionally and encourage them to represent themselves and the club well.
  • Help them practise at home and build a passion for the game.
  • Avoid rewards like money for goals, which can encourage selfish play.
  • Encourage them to watch games, highlights and tutorials to learn.
Matchday rules4 pages

No offsides at U10. All free kicks are direct except for a deliberate header. Retreat line: the team without the ball drops into its own half for a goal kick. Power play: a team losing by four goals can add an extra player.
